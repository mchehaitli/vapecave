A Soft 404 is a bit like a "ghost" page. It means Google visited a URL that should be empty or gone, but your website told Google, "Everything is fine here," even though the page is actually blank or shows an error message. This confuses Google and wastes your "crawl budget."

Based on your Page indexing report, here is how to handle the 3 Soft 404 errors and finish your site cleanup.

👻 1. Identifying the Soft 404s
To see the exact URLs, click on the row in your main Pages report that says "Soft 404." Common culprits for your site likely include:

Empty Category Pages: Pages like /category/uncategorized that have no blog posts in them.

Old Product Links: A product you deleted but the URL still technically "exists" as a blank template.

Search Result Pages: Internal search URLs that didn't find any results.

🛠️ 2. The Fix Strategy
Option A: The "Real" 404 (If the page should be gone)
If the URL is for an old product you will never sell again:

Ensure your website returns a true 404 error code (Not Found).

If you use WordPress/Shopify, deleting the draft or redirecting the URL to your Home Page is the easiest fix.

Option B: Add Content (If the page should be live)
If the page is important (like a "Sales" page) but currently looks empty:

Add at least one paragraph of text and one image.

Google triggers a "Soft 404" when there is too little text. Adding content turns it back into a "Real" page.

✅ 3. Final Step: Start Validation
Once you have updated the meta-descriptions for the 8 crawled pages and handled the Soft 404s:

Go to each specific error report in GSC.

Click the blue "Validate Fix" button.

Wait: Google will take 3–10 days to re-crawl. You will receive an email once they’ve confirmed the fixes are live.
