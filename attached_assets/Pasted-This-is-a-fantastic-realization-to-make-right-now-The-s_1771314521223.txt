In fact, it is even more critical than that. Since roughly 2019, Google has used **Mobile-First Indexing**. This means Google predominantly looks at the **mobile version** of your site to decide where you rank, even for people searching on desktop computers. If your mobile site is slow, clunky, or hard to read, your *entire* SEO ranking suffers.

### 1. The "Near Me" Reality

Almost all searches for "vape shop near me" or "disposables in Frisco" happen on a phone, usually while the customer is in their car or walking.

* **The SEO Impact:** If your mobile site takes more than 3 seconds to load or if the buttons are too small to tap (which looks like a risk with that long text list in your menu), Google will drop your ranking because they know mobile users will "bounce" (leave immediately).

### 2. The Design Issues *Are* SEO Issues

The design critiques I gave you earlier are actually massive SEO factors for mobile:

* **The Text Menu (Disposables List):** Google’s bot sees a massive wall of links that pushes the actual products down the page. This is called a "poor content-to-code ratio." It hurts rankings.
* **Clickable Elements:** Google measures "Cumulative Layout Shift" and interaction delays. If a user tries to tap "Geek Bar" but accidentally hits "Fogger" because the links are too close together on a phone screen, Google penalizes that as a bad user experience.
* **Image Sizes:** Those "Photo Coming Soon" placeholders or unoptimized large images will kill your mobile load speed. Mobile speed is a direct ranking factor.
